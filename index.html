Heritage & History

The Soul of
Mongolian Dairy

Ааруул is among Mongolia's sacred цагаан идээ — "white foods" — dairy products symbolizing purity, hospitality, and the nomadic spirit shaping Central Asia for millennia.

Aaruul · Traditional Dried Curd Shapes

Aaruul has been made by Mongolian nomads for thousands of years. Crafted from the curd left after making airag or from cow, yak, camel, or sheep milk — it is pressed into shape, carved with family motifs, and dried under the open sky.

Each family bears its own traditional mold, passed from mother to daughter, turning a simple food into folk art and genealogy. Visitors often receive a piece carved with the host family's emblem.

Portable, nourishing, and enduring, aaruul traveled the Silk Road in the saddlebags of traders and the provisions of Chinggis Khan's cavalry. Today it remains the first food offered to any guest who crosses a threshold.

What Is Aaruul

A Cheese Unlike
Any Other

Aaruul is not aged like European cheese — it is sun-dried. The curd is pressed firm, shaped by hand or mold, and left to desiccate over days on wooden racks until it is bone-hard and completely shelf-stable.

The result is intensely tangy, faintly sour, and deeply rich. You do not simply eat aaruul — you suck it slowly, letting it soften on the tongue and release its concentrated dairy flavour over minutes.

Its texture ranges from crumbly when fresh-dried to nearly stone-hard after months of curing. Both are prized in different contexts — the fresh for guests, the aged for travel and winter.

Traditional Method

How to Make
Aaruul at Home

Patient, unhurried, and connected to the seasons. This is the cow's milk method practiced across the Mongolian countryside for generations.

Ingredients

Whole milk5 litres
White vinegar or lemon3–4 tbsp
Saltoptional pinch
Cheesecloth1 large piece
Wooden moldor hands
Direct sunshine2–7 days

Best made in summer when milk is richest. Yak or camel milk gives a denser, more pungent result.

01

Boil & Separate the Milk

Pour whole milk into a heavy pot and bring slowly to a gentle boil, stirring occasionally. Once boiling, reduce heat and add vinegar or lemon juice one tablespoon at a time, stirring gently in slow circles. The milk will separate into white curds floating in pale yellowish whey.

Do not stir vigorously — gentle movements preserve larger curd pieces, giving better texture in the finished aaruul.
02

Drain Overnight in Cheesecloth

Line a colander with cheesecloth, ladle in the curds, gather the cloth into a bundle and hang it over a bowl. Drain for at least 2 hours — overnight is traditional. The longer it drains, the firmer and drier your aaruul will become.

Never discard the whey. Mongolians drink it warm, add it to bread dough, or feed it to livestock — nothing goes to waste on the steppe.
03

Press & Shape with Family Molds

Unwrap the drained curd — it should feel like thick, crumbly fresh cheese. Press it firmly into carved wooden molds bearing your family's motif, or form by hand into rounds, diamonds, twisted ropes, or animal shapes. Children learn their family's shapes from early childhood.

Add a pinch of salt for a savory result, or a little sugar for sweetness. Some families press in dried sea buckthorn berries or wild herbs.
04

Sun-Dry on the Ger Roof — 3 to 7 Days

Arrange shaped pieces on a clean wooden board or wire rack placed in full direct sunlight — traditionally on the roof of the ger, where maximum sun and wind reach. Turn pieces once daily. In Mongolia's dry continental climate, aaruul is ready in 3–5 days; in humid climates allow 7 days, or use a 50°C oven with the door slightly ajar.

Ready when the piece makes a hollow sound when tapped and feels bone-dry on the outside. The interior may remain very slightly yielding — that is correct.
05

Store & Present to Guests

Store in a breathable cloth bag or wooden box — never sealed plastic, which traps moisture and causes mold. Properly dried aaruul keeps for a year or more without refrigeration. To present to guests, arrange on a blue silk cloth and offer with both hands extended — the highest gesture of welcome.

Regional Character

Six Expressions
of One Tradition

Across Mongolia's 21 provinces, every family and region makes aaruul differently — distinct milks, shapes, drying times, and flavour profiles.

Six aaruul varieties illustrated

Cow Milk · Common

The most widespread variety. Mild, tangy, creamy white. The everyday snack in every home, school lunchbox, and roadside stall across the country.

Yak Milk · Mountain Luxury

From the Khangai and Altai ranges. Richer and more golden — the aaruul is denser, butterier, more intense. Prized as a seasonal delicacy.

Camel Milk · Gobi Desert

Lower in fat, naturally rich in vitamin C. Camel-milk aaruul is crumbly, faintly salty, with a distinct pungency — a unique product of the southern desert provinces.

Sheep Milk · Herding Provinces

Less common but deeply traditional. Mild when fresh-dried, it develops a sharper, nuttier character with age — favoured by connoisseurs.

Flavoured · Modern Urban

Sugar, sea buckthorn, rose hip, and honey varieties sold in Ulaanbaatar's markets — approachable for newcomers, popular with younger generations.

For Visitors

Finding Aaruul
in Mongolia

Whether staying in a ger camp or passing through Ulaanbaatar, encounters with aaruul await at every turn of the steppe road.

Mongolian hospitality is not a performance — it is a code of honour embedded in daily life. When a nomad family opens their ger door, the first act is always the same: white food, offered with both hands.

To receive aaruul graciously — even a small taste, even if it is not to your liking — is one of the most meaningful things a foreign visitor can do. It signals respect for a tradition that has survived empires and centuries.

🏠

Ger Camp Stays

Book a traditional ger stay and join the host family at sunrise as they make fresh aaruul. Many camps offer hands-on dairy workshops with family molds you can try yourself.

🛒

Naran Tuul Market

Ulaanbaatar's vast outdoor "Black Market" dedicates entire sections to dairy. Dozens of varieties, regions, and ages side by side — sample freely and compare.

🐎

Nomad Family Visits

Operators in Khentii and Arkhangai arrange day visits to genuine herding families. Aaruul is always the first thing offered — accept with both hands.

🎪

Naadam Festival — July

During the national festival, food stalls overflow with traditional treats. Fresh-pressed and stone-hard aged pieces in every shape imaginable, all in one place.

✈️

Taking It Home

Fully dried aaruul clears customs easily and keeps for months without refrigeration. Vacuum-sealed packs are sold at Chinggis Khaan International Airport.

🤝

Cultural Etiquette

Receive with both hands or the right hand. Never outright refuse — set a piece aside gently if needed. Tasting is a gesture of friendship that transcends language.
